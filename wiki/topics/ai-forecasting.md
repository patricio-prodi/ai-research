# AI Forecasting: Methodology and Credibility

**Thesis**: AI capability forecasting is a legitimate empirical discipline, not pure speculation — but requires careful attention to methodology, distributional assumptions, and incentive structures. The [[sources/ai-2027]] project is a serious attempt; its credibility rests on track record and methodology, not just the stature of its authors.

## Why forecasting AI is hard

Forecasting AI capability is harder than forecasting most technologies because:
1. **No prior case study**: we have never built superhuman AI before. There's no base rate.
2. **Recursive self-improvement possibility**: if AI helps build better AI, capability curves may not be smooth.
3. **Discontinuity risk**: emergent capabilities appear suddenly at scale thresholds, making extrapolation unreliable.
4. **Incentive distortion**: lab CEOs have commercial reasons to project capability confidence; safety researchers have reasons to project alarm.

## AI 2027 methodology

From [[sources/ai-2027]]:
- ~25 tabletop exercises (wargames simulating AI development scenarios)
- Feedback from 100+ experts across AI governance and technical work
- Trend extrapolation from current capability curves
- Iterative scenario writing: write period, then next period, then scrap and rewrite

**Track record**: One author's August 2021 blog post successfully predicted:
- Chain-of-thought reasoning
- Inference-time scaling
- AI chip export controls
- $100M training runs

All called >1 year before ChatGPT. This is a meaningful, specific track record — not just "an AI person said AI would improve."

## Interpreting "modal year 2027"

The AI 2027 claim is a **modal estimate**, not a mean or median:
- Modal = most likely single year
- Distribution is right-skewed (long-tail scenarios where AGI takes longer)
- Median is "somewhat longer" than 2027

Common misreading: "AI 2027 predicts AGI in 2027" — technically this means "2027 is the single most probable year, but the expected value is later."

## The CEO consensus signal

Three major lab CEOs (Altman, Hassabis, Amodei) predicting AGI within ~5 years (as of early 2025) is a data point, not proof. But it is notable because:
- These are people with direct access to private capability evaluations.
- They have asymmetric incentives (under-claiming is also costly — investors, talent).
- Their predictions have become more specific and near-term over time.

The convergence across competitors (OpenAI, Google DeepMind, Anthropic) is harder to dismiss than any single CEO's statement.

## Credibility anchors

What makes AI 2027 worth taking seriously:
1. Specific, falsifiable claims with concrete timelines
2. Publicly invites disagreement and alternative scenarios
3. Prize pool for best counter-scenarios
4. Backed by a verifiable prior track record
5. Methodology disclosed (tabletop exercises, expert feedback)

What to remain skeptical about:
1. Ex-OpenAI perspective may have systematic bias toward OpenAI's trajectory
2. Extrapolating from recent capability gains assumes no fundamental barriers ahead
3. "Informed by wargames" ≠ validated predictions

## Yoshua Bengio's endorsement

Bengio — not known for hype — endorsed the project:
> "I highly recommend reading this scenario-type prediction... this type of content can help notice important questions and illustrate the potential impact of emerging risks."

Notably, he endorses it as a useful thinking tool, not as a proven forecast.
